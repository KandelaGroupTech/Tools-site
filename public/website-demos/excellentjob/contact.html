What is a commercial tenant improvement (TI) or office fit-out?

A commercial tenant improvement (TI), also called an office fit-out, is the interior construction that prepares a leased or vacant commercial space for a tenant to occupy and work. At JOB that includes build-outs and renovations of occupied suites and of leasing-ready space, including white-box and spec suite construction.

Scope follows the drawings. It can include partitions, finishes, commercial flooring, paint and wall finishes, selective demolition, and coordination of the trades required to complete the space. Lobbies, corridors, amenity spaces, and other capital improvements are handled as building renovations, separate from the tenant's suite. The client is usually the property manager, owner, architect, or tenant who needs the space delivered inside an operating building.

Does Journey Office Builders build SCIFs in Washington, DC, Northern Virginia, and Maryland?

Yes. Journey Office Builders builds Sensitive Compartmented Information Facilities (SCIFs) in the same Washington, DC, Northern Virginia, and Maryland market as its office and building work, including secure government facilities. SCIF construction is performed to ICD/ICS 705 standards, and the construction team supports the owner's accreditation process from the start of the build.

A SCIF is not a standard office fit-out. ICD 705 sets the physical and technical security requirements the construction has to meet. Accreditation stays the facility owner's process. JOB is SAM registered. Our federal identifiers are UEI KCKWP7QAF157 and CAGE 8V9X6. Classification, accreditation roles, and construction scope are confirmed in the quote for that facility. How long does a typical office tenant improvement take?

We do not publish a guaranteed duration for an office tenant improvement. On the quote request form, clients describe the window they need as ASAP, 1-3 months, 3-6 months, or 6+ months. Those are request categories, not a promise that a job will finish inside them.

What moves a project from one window to another:

  • Scope. Replacing commercial flooring, repainting, or selective demolition in one suite is a shorter job than a full office fit-out with new partitions, MEP coordination, and a white-box or spec suite.
  • Occupied buildings. Work beside tenants is sequenced around access windows, protection of adjacent suites, and building rules. Calendar time is often longer than crew time.
  • Permits. Review in DC, Virginia, or Maryland sits on the critical path and is not controlled by the contractor.
  • Design and materials. Incomplete drawings, long-lead finishes, and after-hours work rules change the schedule even when the area looks similar.

The duration that matters is the one stated in the quote for that address and scope. We acknowledge a request within the hour and return a quote within 24-48 hours, so the schedule is tied to the actual building.
