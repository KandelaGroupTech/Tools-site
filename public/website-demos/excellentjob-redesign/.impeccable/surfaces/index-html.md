## Direction contract
THESIS: The site is the branded hoarding wall JOB stands up on an occupied floor: a clean printed partition that says who is working, how to reach them, and hides the mess until the finished space is revealed. It refuses the category default of a darkened photo carousel with centered headline, counter stats and icon cards.
OWN-WORLD: Printed vinyl wall panels on a cool primer-white ground: full-bleed panels of logo maroon #7C0A2B and skyline black #232120 meeting at visible panel seams and fastener dots; extra-condensed heavy grotesk (Archivo, condensed widths) set huge like site signage; normal-width Archivo for reading. Square corners, no shadows except the depth of the viewing window. Wayfinding plates (suite-number style) carry nav and labels.
STORY: A property manager sees JOB's wall, reads the promise (answered within the hour, quoted in 24 to 48 hours, invoice matches the estimate), looks through the window at finished work, trusts the names on the wall, and calls or requests a quote.
FIRST VIEWPORT: Full-width hoarding: left two-thirds a maroon panel with the headline in giant condensed caps and the three-part response promise as stacked signage lines; right third a black panel with the phone number set large and a Request a Quote plate. A rectangular viewing window cut through the seam shows rotating finished-project photos. Primary action: the phone and quote plate, top right, visible on mobile without scrolling.
FORM: Construction hoarding / occupied-floor wall wrap, position 6 of 7 on the ordered list; seed key c80d663c. Signature interaction: the "wall pull-back" before/after reveal (drag the hoarding panel aside to uncover the finished space) on home and projects. Motion grammar: panels slide on a horizontal track with exponential ease-out; nothing else animates.
FINISH: unreviewed and undocumented is unfinished; this build ends with the finish review, the verdict, DESIGN.md, and every shipping raster carrying its provenance
